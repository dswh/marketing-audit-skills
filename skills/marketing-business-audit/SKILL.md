# Business audit

## Inputs

Website, client notes, offer, known results and available resources. Supply actual file contents, accessible files or pasted material. Relevant prior reports can be replaced by equivalent raw context. Keep supplied facts, historical results and authorized planning assumptions separate.


Start with discovery before selecting marketing tactics. Use supplied call notes or prepare 6-8 questions about the business, product, buyer, revenue path, current process, problem, past results, resources and expected outcome. The client supplies facts; the marketer organizes them and agrees the brief together. Never invent interview answers.
Capture the customer's problem, the offer, who buys, how the business earns revenue, the price or revenue model, proof, current channels and buying path.
Name the business's stated growth problem separately from any measured marketing issue. Request evidence for causal claims.
Turn discovery into a working meeting: name who brings the active acquisition asset and destination, a completed customer cohort, and the matching order/refund/cost records. Assign unresolved discrepancies to proposed roles until the client names people.
Show actual versus modeled spend, customers and contribution side by side. Request contribution per buyer after refunds and variable delivery costs, excluding costs already counted in the campaign. Calculate the full-budget break-even contribution per buyer; ask finance for an acceptable acquisition-cost ceiling. Separate whole-campaign affordability from the economics of the next release.
Define the primary customer action and how it is counted. Add a quality or downstream-value measure, baseline, agreed target, available budget, team time and constraints. A baseline means previous actual results; a target means the proposed outcome for the next period. Keep missing history explicit. With user authorization, complete a planning model using labeled assumptions and calculate it consistently. Do not mix a form-navigation note into a baseline or target field.
For a registration business, map through the actual paid outcome: ads, landing visits, completed registrations, attendance, paid offer/follow-up and purchase. The actual ad landing page may differ from a homepage route. Distinguish form starts, completed registrations, attendance and payment; record who owns each step. For ecommerce, distinguish orders, margin and repeat purchases. For software, distinguish demos, activation and paid accounts. For local services, distinguish enquiries and completed bookings.
Keep the scope to one offer. End with at most three unanswered questions that could change the goal or scope.
When useful, retain an original screenshot of the offer and primary CTA with its URL, viewport, date and visible state. Pass it to marketing-journey-audit for annotation; reuse captures instead of repeating them. Keep private account or customer details out of shareable evidence.

## Outputs

In discovery mode (or the legacy goal mode), create Discovery-Notes.html and update section discovery. This is a working record, not automatically a separately sold deliverable.
In context mode, create Business-Context.html and update section business-context. Save the same shared facts to .agents/marketing-business-context.md.
With no mode specified, start with discovery. If enough context is supplied, also draft the working brief for agreement.

## Working rules

Use the materials the user supplies. Read .agents/marketing-business-context.md and the actual existing reports when available. A filename alone is not the report contents.
Ask once for missing information that could change the result. The user may proceed with unknowns.
Research permitted public pages when browsing is available. Record the URL and date checked. A supplied URL is not evidence that it was opened. If access fails, ask for page text or record a research task.
Keep observed facts, customer statements, provider claims, assumptions and proposals distinct. Preserve product labels and sample limits. Do not invent historical metrics, customer quotes, people, sources or actual performance. When the user authorizes planning assumptions, label each as an assumption, show its source and calculations, and keep it separate from actuals. Never call an assumed rate a market benchmark.
Use plain words, short bullets, a useful diagram or compact comparison, and a small source list. No decorative scores or unsupported earnings claims.
Make the output a decision blueprint, not an essay. Give each section one useful visual artifact and one decision. Use short labels and content-fit cards. Put detailed evidence behind the main reading view. Never replace missing actuals with modeled numbers or use an invented score to make the report look complete.
When evidence cannot locate the constraint, recommend the smallest investigation that can. A supplied budget or an attractive screenshot does not justify a paid pilot. Name the proposed owner, missing evidence and release condition instead.
Create local draft files only. Sharing, publishing, account access, messages and spending require their own authorization.

## Report continuity

Keep each working output understandable with its own context. Working notes are not automatically separate client deliverables or paid services. Add or update its matching section in Marketing-Strategy-and-Action-Plan.html while preserving all other sections and sources. Start the master if absent. Missing sections remain missing, not invented.
If files cannot be written, return the report content and intended filename. Mark owners, targets, budgets and copy as proposed until agreed.
